# Road running (cestni tek)

Part of the [coaching method](../coaching-method.md). Source numbers refer to its section 12.

**What decides performance:** VO₂max, the speed at LT2, and running economy. For the half marathon and marathon, how long the athlete can hold a pace near LT2 (half) or between LT1 and LT2 (marathon) matters most, plus fuelling in the marathon.

## Season
| Months | Phase | Main training |
|---|---|---|
| Nov–Dec | transition, then base | easy running, strength, other sports |
| Jan–Feb | base / build | long runs, threshold |
| Mar–May | spring races | e.g. 10 km and half marathons |
| Jun–Jul | base | volume, strides, hills |
| Aug–Sep | build | race-specific sessions |
| Sep–Oct | autumn races | e.g. Ljubljana Marathon (late October) |

Most athletes pick one marathon per year at most; plan 16–20 weeks for it (base, build, peak, 3-week taper).

## Key sessions
Lead metric: pace for Z3–Z5 on flat ground; HR caps for Z1–Z2.

| Session | Prescription | Zone | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long run | 75–150 min easy; for marathon build, the last 20–40 min at marathon pace | Z2 (end Z3) | aerobic base, endurance |
| Threshold intervals | 4–5 × 6–8 min or 3 × 10 min, 1–2 min jog between | Z4 | speed at LT2 |
| Continuous tempo | 20–30 min | Z3–Z4 | half marathon pace |
| VO₂max intervals | 5–6 × 3–4 min at about 5 km race pace, equal time jogging; or 8–12 × 400 m | Z5 | VO₂max |
| Marathon pace | 2–3 × 20–30 min at marathon pace inside a 90–120 min run | Z3 | marathon specifics, fuelling practice |
| Hill sprints | 6–10 × 10 s steep uphill, full walk-back recovery | neuromuscular | power, economy, low injury risk |
| Strides | 4–6 × 20 s after an easy run, 1–2× a week | — | form, economy |

Pace targets follow the athlete's zones; equivalent race paces can be cross-checked with Daniels' tables [35].

## Strength and technique
- **Strength** (2× a week in base, 1–2× in season): squats, deadlifts or hip thrusts, single-leg work, calf raises (to protect the Achilles and calf), core. Heavy, 3–4 sets of 4–8 reps; it improves running economy [31].
- **Plyometrics** (1× a week): pogo jumps, skipping, bounding; 40–80 contacts.
- **Technique:** cadence and posture cues in strides; no forced change of footstrike.

## Specific risks and rules
- Running causes the most overuse injuries of all sports here: HR-02 and HR-03 always apply, and the long run is at most ~35 % of weekly running time (convention).
- Shoes: one shoe change at a time, not in race week.
- New runners start with run-walk sessions (e.g. 1 min run / 1 min walk × 10) and progress by time, not distance.
